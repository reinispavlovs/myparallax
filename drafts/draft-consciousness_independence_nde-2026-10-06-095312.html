Independent evaluation of evidence categories

The percentages supplied in the dataset (1.1% supports_veridical; 0.0% null_result; 8.0% neuro_mechanism; 80.7% inconclusive) are treated as a rough corpus-level signal, not as a peer-reviewed effect size. The raw tally also lists observational_signal as 9 and inconclusive as 71. Category boundaries are described in the supplied skeptic critique as inherently fuzzy.

Claim status key used in this draft

  • Proven: independently corroborated, methodologically adequate, and not in material dispute within this dataset. No item in the supplied corpus meets this bar.
  • Supported: at least one classified source is consistent with the claim, but the claim is not established beyond that classification.
  • Suggestive: recurring observational reports or related phenomenological comparisons that warrant analysis as data.
  • Unresolved: competing explanations remain open; the corpus does not decide among them.
  • Unsubstantiated: a conclusion that would require evidence this corpus does not supply.

Supports_veridical (count 1; 1.1%)

The dataset classifies one abstract as supports_veridical: a rare-case report whose title refers to a distressing near-death experience with veridical perceptions during coma. That classification is consistent with a case-level report of anomalous perception. It is not treated here as proof of non-local mind, nor as a generalizable effect. Status for a general claim of veridical perception during coma: supported only at single-case classification level; otherwise unresolved.

Null_result (count 0; 0.0%)

The dataset lists no classified null-result items. That is a property of this LLM-labelled corpus, not a finding that null results are absent from the wider literature. Status: unresolved. Absence of classified nulls in this file is not evidence of an effect.

Neuro_mechanism (count 7; 8.0%)

Seven classified items are consistent with brain-based accounts of near-death or dying-related experience. Titles in this group refer to brain twilight zones, REM-sleep intrusion, migraine aura as a predictor of near-death experiences, arousal-system contribution, a dying-moment dream hypothesis, and neurochemical models based on semantic similarity of written reports. These sources suggest competing physiological hypotheses (including hypoxia-related, temporal-lobe, REM-intrusion, and neurochemical accounts named in the skeptic critique). They do not, on the supplied evidence, exclude non-local interpretations, nor do they settle mechanism. Status: supported as a family of competing hypotheses; unresolved as an exclusive explanation.

Observational_signal (count 9)

Nine items are classified as observational_signal. Titles in this group include cardiopulmonary resuscitation-induced consciousness, a near-death-experience overview, a scoping review of end-of-life experiences and implications for the mind-brain relationship, terminal lucidity case material, and comparative thematic analyses of DMT or 5-methoxy-DMT reports with near-death phenomenology. Independent recurring reports are treated as an observational dataset. Testimony is not proof and is not zero evidence. Status: suggestive as a phenomenological and clinical reporting pattern; unsubstantiated as proof of independence of consciousness.

Inconclusive (count 71; 80.7%)

Most of the corpus is classified inconclusive. Many listed titles appear only loosely related, or unrelated, to non-local mind or reported perceptual anomalies (for example, school-zone speed compliance, ovine coenurosis, rodenticide exposure in a leopard, dental-matrix delivery, and several metabolic or oncologic papers). That pattern is consistent with a noisy retrieval-and-classification pipeline rather than with a focused evidence base. Inconclusive is not used here as a dumping category for non-mainstream claims solely because mainstream consensus is absent: the dataset already assigned separate labels to supports_veridical, neuro_mechanism, and observational_signal. Status of the inconclusive majority: unresolved and, for many titles, thematically uninformative to the target question.

Reported perceptual anomalies (near-death, end-of-life, terminal lucidity, CPR-related consciousness, and related phenomenology): suggestive as an observational dataset. The study set reports recurring first-person and clinical descriptions. Recurrence is data. Recurrence is not adjudication.
