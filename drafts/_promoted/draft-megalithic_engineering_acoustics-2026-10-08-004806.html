Independent category evaluation

Established

The intensity reflection factor for a normally incident acoustic wave at a boundary between two media, written (Z1-Z2)^2/(Z1+Z2)^2, is standard impedance physics. A large mismatch between characteristic impedances is consistent with a high-reflection boundary. The dataset labels the computed case HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER and reports an energy reflection of 99.99 [source:illustrative_model] percent at 115.0 Hz, with an acoustic pressure of 28.99 [source:illustrative_model] Pa. Those three figures are illustrative-model outputs, not field measurements from a megalithic site.

Supported

The supplied arXiv excerpt reports that strongly enhanced transmission through a single subwavelength slit is discussed when the slit boundary is represented by an effective impedance, rather than only through periodic or multi-aperture arrays. That claim is attributed to the excerpt. It is consistent with a research programme on boundary-impedance engineering. The excerpt does not, in the text provided, assign this mechanism to granite architecture or to any archaeological culture.

Suggestive

If joints, slits, or cavity mouths in stonework were to present a tunable boundary impedance, the physics reported in the excerpt would be relevant to hypotheses about megalithic acoustics and structural resonance. The illustrative high-reflection result is also consistent with an untreated air–stone interface behaving as a barrier to airborne sound. These are hypothesis-level links only. They do not by themselves rank the archaeological reading as supported.

Granite impedance, airborne sound, and resonance

Project Parallax does not treat missing consensus as a substitute for a negative finding. It also does not treat a standard reflection calculation as a substitute for a positive finding about design intent. Two statements can be held together: (1) an air–granite face is expected, under ordinary mismatch physics, to look like a high-reflection barrier for airborne waves, which is consistent with the illustrative HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER status; (2) extraordinary transmission, as discussed in the slit-impedance excerpt, is a different boundary-value problem in which the wall response is engineered so that a subwavelength opening does not behave like that barrier.

Structural resonance of a granite body is a statement about the solid, not about the reflected airborne fraction. The illustrative model, by the model note, is the mismatch formula only. It does not establish energy delivery into the stone. The skeptic critique is aligned with that limit: without an extra coupling mechanism such as seismic strain or a closed Helmholtz geometry, airborne waves at the listed model point are not shown to overcome stone mass. That is a coupling gap in the present dataset, not a tribunal finding that megalithic acoustic reports are false.
