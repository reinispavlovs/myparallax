On the numbers as supplied, the large majority of the corpus (87.5%; 77 of 88) is labeled inconclusive. That distribution is consistent with a retrieval set in which most abstracts do not clearly support a veridical, non-local interpretation and also do not clearly isolate a single neural mechanism. It is not a basis for treating non-local mind models as shown.

A small slice (3.4%; 3 of 88) is labeled supports_veridical in the dataset. The three titles so labeled are: a 2026 case discussion of a distressing near-death experience with reported veridical perceptions during coma; a 2025 scoping review of end-of-life experiences and implications for the mind-brain relationship; and a 2013 case study of terminal lucidity (the death of Anna Katharina Ehmer). Those labels are LLM classifications of abstracts in the supplied list. This draft does not treat the labels as verified evidence that perception occurred independently of brain function.

Another slice (9.1%; 8 of 88) is labeled neuro_mechanism. Titles in that bin include work on brain “twilight zones,” associations of near-death experiences with REM-sleep intrusion and with migraine aura, a 2006 paper asking whether the arousal system contributes to near-death experience, a 2026 “dying-moment dream” hypothesis, a comparative analysis of a near-death experience and a subsequent 5-MeO-DMT experience, and a large-scale semantic-similarity study of neurochemical models of near-death reports. Those items are consistent with accounts that locate anomalous perception in known or candidate neural and neurochemical processes. They do not, on the supplied metadata alone, rule out other models, and they are not here described as conclusive.

The dataset reports 0.0% null_result (0 of 88). In an illustrative LLM-labeled corpus, a zero count of null results is consistent with retrieval, labeling, or category-boundary effects. It should not be read as an empirical demonstration that null findings are absent from the wider literature.

Competing interpretations therefore remain open in this draft:

  • Non-local or independence models would expect at least some well-documented veridical perception when cortical function is severely reduced. The supplied corpus contains only three abstracts labeled in that direction, and those labels are unverified.
  • Neural-substrate models in the labeled set include hypoxia-related transitional brain states, REM intrusion, migraine aura, arousal-system activity, a terminal dream hypothesis, and overlap with psychedelic phenomenology. The eight neuro_mechanism labels are also unverified as to the correctness of the bin.
  • The skeptic critique in the dataset states that a pattern of reported near-death perceptions does not by itself distinguish those families of explanation.

A further caution, still without new numbers: many titles in the inventory appear only loosely related to independence of consciousness (for example, veterinary stunning, school-zone speed, clavicular anatomy, ovine coenurosis, or oncology case reports).
