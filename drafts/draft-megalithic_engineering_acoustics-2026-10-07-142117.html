Independent evidence-category evaluation

Proven (within ordinary physics, not archaeology)

The energy-reflection expression given in the model note, (Z1-Z2)^2/(Z1+Z2)^2, is the standard intensity reflection factor for a normal-incidence impedance mismatch. That formula is ordinary acoustics. It does not by itself establish anything about prehistoric design.

Within the supplied illustrative model, a frequency of 115.0 Hz, an acoustic pressure of 28.98879093718812 Pa, and an energy reflection of 99.98755114530057 percent are reported, with boundary status HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER. Those outputs are consistent with a large impedance contrast, such as is expected for airborne sound meeting a much denser solid. They are not supported as on-site megalithic measurements, because the data type is illustrative_model.

Suggestive (physics analogy only)

The arXiv fragment is consistent with the idea that a single subwavelength aperture can, in engineered laboratory conditions, show strongly enhanced transmission if the boundary is described by an effective impedance rather than by a simple rigid-wall assumption. If megalithic seams, slots, or passages were later shown to present comparable boundary conditions, that physics could become relevant. No such megalithic boundary measurements are in this dataset. The analogy is therefore suggestive at most.

Granite impedance and the high-reflection barrier

The illustrative model labels the interface a HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER and reports nearly total energy reflection. That result is consistent with a severe airborne-to-stone impedance mismatch. It does not suggest easy injection of airborne acoustic energy into granite volume. The skeptic critique states the same point: the reflection figure is standard impedance physics, not archaeological proof of intent, and airborne waves alone cannot overcome stone mass without an additional coupling mechanism such as seismic strain or a closed Helmholtz geometry.

For a tribunal, this cuts both ways. High reflection is consistent with interiors that could retain airborne sound if a space is enclosed. High reflection is not consistent with a simple story in which outdoor or loosely coupled airborne sound “enters the stone” and resonates the structure as a bulk oscillator. Any resonance hypothesis that needs energy in the solid, rather than in an air cavity, still requires a coupling path that this dataset does not provide.

Structural resonance: what can and cannot be said

The dataset contains one frequency point (115.0 Hz) and one pressure point (28.98879093718812 Pa). A single illustrative pair does not map a modal spectrum, a Q factor, a granite stiffness, or a chamber volume. No statement is made here that any monument resonates at 115.0 Hz.

Boundary-impedance engineering, as reported in the truncated source fragment, concerns transmission through a slit when the boundary response is an effective impedance. That is a possible language for discussing joints and apertures. It is not evidence that megalithic joints were so engineered. Project Parallax does not dismiss that hypothesis merely because it is non-mainstream; it also does not promote it beyond the evidence category it currently occupies, which is unresolved, with only a suggestive physics analogy.

Failsafe critique (retained)

The supplied skeptic critique is adopted as a failsafe, not as a veto on inquiry: the approximately 99.99 percent acoustic energy reflection figure is standard impedance physics, not archaeological proof of intent. Airborne acoustic waves alone cannot overcome stone mass without an additional coupling mechanism (seismic strain or a closed Helmholtz geometry). This draft therefore does not treat high reflection as proof of purpose-built megalithic acoustics.
