Scope and method

Project Parallax evaluates evidence categories independently. Absence of academic consensus is not treated as evidence of falsehood. Presence of testimony is not treated as evidence of truth. Large numbers of independent reports, when they exist, are data and may be analysed statistically; this draft contains no such report census. Non-mainstream claims are not collapsed into an “inconclusive” bin solely because mainstream consensus is absent. Claims are labelled as proven, supported, suggestive, unresolved, or unsubstantiated.

Attributed source text (cosmogenic nuclides)

The study reports an exploration of the idea that detectable excursions in 26Al may arise from direct deposition by any bolide, and that excursions in 14C and 10Be abundances in the atmosphere may result from long-period comet impacts. The same text states that this framing is very different from the usual processes of production by cosmic rays. The passage is offered as a possible test of the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis, not as a completed verification.

On the tribunal scale this strand is suggestive as a proposed observational test and unresolved as to whether such excursions have been shown, in this dataset, to record a long-period comet or other bolide at the given marker. Nothing in the supplied extract is treated as proven production physics or as a unique fingerprint of a named impactor.

The cautionary check states that the mathematical cyclical model is illustrative, not a proven correlation mechanism, and that a sediment platinum peak near the same marker is an external-impact issue separate from the orbital-mechanics cycle. This draft follows that separation.

  • Proven: No claim in this dataset meets this bar. A found source record and an illustration are not proof of cyclical catastrophism or of orbital forcing.
  • Supported: That a source record exists for the cosmogenic-nuclide discussion (arxiv_source_found true), and that the quantitative cycle is explicitly labelled illustrative_model.
  • Suggestive: The reported idea that bolide deposition or long-period comet impacts could leave unusual 26Al, 14C, or 10Be signatures, distinct from ordinary cosmic-ray production.
  • Unresolved: Whether any such nuclide excursion, if later measured, would identify a comet rather than another bolide; whether the 25920-year illustration has a real orbital-eccentricity mechanism; whether CYCLE_SYNCHRONIZED describes nature or only the sinusoid.
  • Unsubstantiated: Treating the sinusoid as a geological measurement; treating boundary_status as an observed orbital-flux peak; folding a sediment platinum peak and the orbital illustration into one conclusion.

Impact-related sediment chemistry, if discussed elsewhere, remains a separate evidence category from orbital mechanics. This draft does not use the platinum remark as a measured concentration, because no concentration is in the dataset, and it does not use that remark to validate the cycle.
