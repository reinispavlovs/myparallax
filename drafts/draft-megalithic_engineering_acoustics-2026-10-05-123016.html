Illustrative model of impedance mismatch

The dataset applies the standard acoustic energy-reflection expression (Z1-Z2)^2/(Z1+Z2)^2. At a stated frequency of 115.0 Hz it returns an acoustic pressure of 28.98879093718812 Pa and an energy reflection of 99.98755114530057 percent, with boundary status HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER. These figures are labelled illustrative_model. They are consistent with a large impedance contrast, such as is expected at an ordinary air–stone interface, producing near-total reflection of airborne acoustic energy.

The same dataset therefore contains two different acoustic pictures that should not be collapsed: the illustrative mismatch model reports a high-reflection barrier, whereas the literature extract reports strongly enhanced transmission when boundary impedance is engineered. That contrast is consistent with the need for an additional boundary, geometric, or coupling mechanism if transmission into or through massive stone is claimed. It does not by itself identify what that mechanism would be at a megalithic site.

Supported

Suggestive

The study reports that engineering the effective boundary impedance of a single subwavelength slit can be associated with strongly enhanced transmission. That is suggestive that transmission is not fixed solely by bulk mismatch and that boundary response can matter. Whether megalithic joints, cavities, or granite faces were organised as such boundaries is not shown by this dataset.

Failsafe check as supplied
