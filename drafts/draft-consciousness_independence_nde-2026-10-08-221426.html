Category-by-category evaluation

The following readings treat classifier labels as labels, not as settled ontology. Wording is intentionally non-final.

Supported (within the classifier scheme): supports_veridical (10; 7.6%)

Ten abstracts were labelled supports_veridical. Titles in that bin include case reports and commentaries on veridical perception during near-death or coma states (for example Sepioni, Metastasio, and Monti, 2026; Ring and Lawrence, 1993; Smit, 2008; Smit and Rivas, 2010; Rivas and Smit, 2013, listed twice in the citation list; Sartori, Badham, and Fenwick, 2006; Escolà-Gascón and colleagues, 2026; Lake, 2017; Facco and Agrillo, 2012). The classifier assignment is consistent with a minority strand of the corpus that reports corroborated or apparently extra-bodily perception. That assignment does not establish independence of consciousness, and presence of testimony is not evidence of truth. It also does not license dismissal: testimony is not zero evidence, and these items were not forced into inconclusive merely because they sit outside a mainstream consensus.

Unsubstantiated as a null of the whole field: null_result (1; 0.8%)

One abstract was labelled null_result: Augustine, 2007, “Does paranormal perception occur in near-death experiences?”. A single null-labelled item is not a field-wide refutation. It is one classified document among 131.

Suggestive competing account: neuro_mechanism (46; 35.1%)

This is the largest bin. Abstracts labelled neuro_mechanism include REM-intrusion and migraine-aura work (Kondziella and colleagues, 2019 and 2023; Nelson and colleagues, 2006), dying-brain gamma and coupling papers (Xu and colleagues, 2023; Mashour and colleagues, 2024), out-of-body models tied to the temporoparietal junction (Blanke and colleagues, 2005), DMT and related neurochemical comparisons (Martial and colleagues, 2019; Potts, 2012), and explicit neuroscientific models of near-death experience (Martial and colleagues, 2025; Romand and Ehret, 2022). The skeptic critique in the dataset states that a consistent pattern of reported near-death perceptions does not by itself distinguish non-local consciousness from common neurological substrates such as hypoxia, temporal lobe activation, or REM intrusion. That caution is retained here. These papers suggest brain-based routes that can generate similar phenomenology; they do not, on this aggregate alone, close the question of residual or non-local awareness.

Suggestive observational dataset: observational_signal (40; 30.5%)

Forty abstracts were labelled observational_signal. This bin includes awareness-during-resuscitation reporting (Parnia and colleagues, 2023, AWARE-II), out-of-body phenomenology, terminal lucidity and end-of-life dreams and visions (including Nahm and Greyson, 2013; multiple 2023–2026 lucidity case collections and scoping reviews), ICU and cardiac-arrest near-death scoping reviews, meditation-induced near-death reports (Van Gordon and colleagues, 2018), and DMT/near-death comparative phenomenology (Michael, Luke, and Robinson, 2023 and 2025). Independent recurring reports are treated as an observational dataset. They are suggestive of a stable experiential cluster (near-death, out-of-body, and end-of-life lucidity reports) that is large enough to analyse. They are not, by themselves, a demonstration that mind is independent of brain.

Unresolved: inconclusive (34; 26.0%)

Thirty-four abstracts were labelled inconclusive, including scale-construction and method papers (Greyson and colleagues, 2025, veridical Near-Death Experience Scale), several cardiac-arrest consciousness and CPR-induced consciousness clinical papers, definitional pieces, and mixed or feasibility studies (including Parnia and colleagues, 2025, on consciousness in deep hypothermic circulatory arrest). Inconclusive here means the classifier did not assign a directional evidential role. It does not mean the underlying reports are empty, and it does not mean non-mainstream claims were parked here solely for lack of consensus.

Claim status for Project Parallax (tribunal, not gatekeeper)

  • Established in this draft: only the retrieval and tally figures given in the dataset (325 retrieved, 194 excluded, 131 analysed; database split 19 / 27 / 85; category counts 10 / 1 / 46 / 40 / 34). The existence of a classified literature on near-death, out-of-body, and end-of-life lucidity reports is established as a bibliographic fact of this corpus, not as a metaphysical conclusion.
  • Supported (classifier-supported, not ontologically settled): a minority subset of 10 abstracts is labelled as supporting veridical or non-local readings. That is a supported strand inside this aggregate, not an established finding that consciousness is independent of the brain.
  • Suggestive: 40 observational_signal items plus the sheer recurrence of near-death, out-of-body, and terminal-lucidity reports. Large numbers of independent reports are data. Terminal lucidity and awareness-during-resuscitation reports are suggestive that some experiential claims survive as a research target even when explanatory models diverge.
  • Unresolved: whether those reports track non-local mind, dying-brain physiology, REM intrusion, expectancy, or some mixture. The 46 neuro_mechanism items and the 10 supports_veridical items name competing explanations that this corpus does not adjudicate. Category boundaries are fuzzy, as the skeptic critique states.
  • Unsubstantiated: reading 7.6% as a causal effect size for non-local consciousness; treating LLM abstract labels as a substitute for case-level corroboration; treating absence of consensus as falsehood; treating testimony as sufficient truth. None of those moves is warranted by the dataset.

The study reports a corpus in which neuro_mechanism (35.1%) and observational_signal (30.5%) together dominate, inconclusive is about a quarter (26.0%), supports_veridical is 7.6%, and null_result is 0.8%. That distribution is consistent with an active, contested literature rather than with a single settled model of mind.
