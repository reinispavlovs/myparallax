Non-local mind models versus reported anomalies

Non-local or independence-of-consciousness models treat some near-death, terminal-lucidity, and related reports as possible evidence that mind is not fully identical with presently measurable brain states. The supplied corpus does not settle that model.

  • Established in this draft: only the intake facts (103 retrieved, 69 excluded, 34 analysed; pubmed 21; semantic_scholar 13) and the raw tallies listed above.
  • Supported: that a substantial observational-signal cluster (17 abstracts) and a neuro-mechanism cluster (7 abstracts) both appear after a relevance gate.
  • Suggestive: that at least one classified abstract is placed in supports_veridical, and that terminal lucidity and end-of-life phenomenology recur across hospital, hospice, pediatric, and historical case titles.
  • Unresolved: whether veridical or lucid reports require a non-local mind, or whether hypoxia, temporal lobe activation, REM intrusion, dream-like dying cognition, or neurochemical analogues are sufficient. The skeptic critique states that a consistent pattern of reported near-death perceptions does not by itself distinguish those explanations.
  • Unsubstantiated: any claim that consciousness has been shown to operate independently of the brain, or that the 2.9 share is an effect size for veridical perception in the general population.

Category boundaries are described in the dataset as inherently fuzzy. Several titles sit near those borders (for example, DMT–NDE comparisons labelled observational_signal versus DMT thematic analyses labelled inconclusive; lucidity case series labelled observational_signal versus lucidity terminology papers labelled inconclusive). Fuzzy borders are a reason for caution, not a reason to erase the observational bin.
