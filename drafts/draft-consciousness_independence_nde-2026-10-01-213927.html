Draft reading of the aggregate (non-local mind models)

Non-local or independence-of-consciousness models typically treat some reported perceptual anomalies—veridical near-death perceptions, out-of-body reports, terminal lucidity, and related end-of-life experiences—as possible evidence that awareness is not fully identical with ongoing, local brain function. The present dataset does not test that hypothesis experimentally. It reports only how 103 abstracts were labeled.

On the figures given, 4 of 103 items (3.9%) were labeled supports_veridical, 8 of 103 (7.8%) neuro_mechanism, 91 of 103 (88.3%) inconclusive, and 0 of 103 (0.0%) null_result. The large inconclusive share is consistent with a literature mix in which many retrieved records are clinically adjacent (coma, resuscitation, EEG, anesthesia, or end-of-life care) without adjudicating whether reported perceptions are extra-cranial. The zero null_result tally should not be read as absence of negative evidence in the wider literature; it is only the LLM label count in this corpus.

Titles labeled supports_veridical in the dataset include a case discussion of a distressing near-death experience with reported veridical perceptions during coma, a scoping review of end-of-life experiences and implications for the mind-brain relationship, and two terminal-lucidity case discussions. Those labels suggest that a small minority of retrieved abstracts were read as friendly to veridical or independence-relevant reports. They do not show that the underlying events were independently corroborated, nor that non-local models are required.

Titles labeled neuro_mechanism in the dataset include work linking near-death experiences to REM-sleep intrusion and migraine aura, cognitive correlates of spontaneous out-of-body experience, a dissociative-disorder case framed as astral projection, a dying-moment dream hypothesis, comparative analyses of DMT or 5-MeO-DMT phenomenology with near-death reports, and a large-scale semantic-similarity study of neurochemical models of near-death experiences. That subset is consistent with accounts that locate the phenomenology in known brain states (REM intrusion, temporal-lobe instability, psychedelic-like signaling, or a terminal dream-like process). The dataset does not quantify how well those mechanisms fit individual cases.

Inspection of the citation list, without adding counts, also shows many titles whose topics are remote from non-local mind models (for example emergency surgery, livestock stunning, veterinary EEG, oncology, and unrelated public-health papers). That pattern is consistent with noisy retrieval and with the supplied warning that this is an illustrative meta-analysis of LLM-classified abstracts rather than a curated evidence base.

Limitations for Project Parallax

The supplied skeptic critique should govern interpretation: this is a statistical aggregate of 103 abstracts classified by an LLM, not a hand-verified systematic review. Fuzzy labels mean that supports_veridical, neuro_mechanism, and inconclusive can overlap on the same paper. Reported perceptual anomalies can be read as consistent with either non-local mind models or ordinary neural mechanisms; the percentages given here are a rough corpus-level signal, not a peer-reviewed effect size.

Further human fact-check would need to re-read full texts, drop off-topic retrievals, and separate case narrative from controlled test. Until that review, independence-of-consciousness claims remain an open research question rather than a result of this draft.
