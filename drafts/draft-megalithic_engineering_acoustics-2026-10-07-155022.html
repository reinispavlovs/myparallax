Tribunal method

Project Parallax treats this file as an evidence tribunal, not as an academic gatekeeper. Evidence categories are evaluated independently. Absence of academic consensus is not treated as evidence of falsehood. Presence of testimony is not treated as evidence of truth. Large numbers of independent reports, when they exist, are data and may be analysed statistically. Non-mainstream interpretations are not collapsed into an inconclusive verdict solely because mainstream consensus is absent. Claims are labelled as proven, supported, suggestive, unresolved, or unsubstantiated according to what this dataset actually contains.

This draft does not use verbs of final proof. Where a source is cited, wording follows the form “the study reports,” “is consistent with,” or “suggests.”

Independent evaluation by evidence category

Category A. Air–stone impedance mismatch as ordinary acoustics

Claim class: supported as an illustrative application of a standard formula; not proven as a site measurement.

The energy-reflection percentage 99.98755114530057 is consistent with a large impedance contrast between air and a dense solid when the named mismatch expression is applied. The dataset itself calls this an illustrative model. High reflection at an air–granite interface is therefore treated as ordinary boundary physics, not as a distinctive megalithic signature. The boundary label HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER restates that modelling outcome. It does not, on this evidence, establish builder intent, chamber function, or ritual use.

Category B. Extraordinary acoustic transmission and boundary-impedance engineering

Claim class: suggestive as a laboratory or analytical acoustics result reported in the supplied arXiv context; unresolved as a description of granite monuments.

The study reports that a single subwavelength slit can support strongly enhanced transmission when boundary response is treated as an effective impedance. That claim, as given, concerns engineered slits and reduced analytical models. The dataset does not map slit width, wall impedance, or transmission coefficients onto any named megalith, passage, or granite block. Transfer of the extraordinary-transmission result onto megalithic architecture therefore remains unresolved. Absence of a completed archaeological consensus on that transfer is not used here as proof that the transfer is false; the gap is simply that this dataset does not contain the coupling geometry or the field data that would be required to support it.

Skeptic critique (verbatim sense, English)

Failsafe check: The 99.99% acoustic energy reflection figure is standard impedance physics, not archaeological proof of intent. Airborne acoustic waves alone cannot overcome stone mass without an additional coupling mechanism (seismic strain or a closed Helmholtz geometry).

Tribunal reading: the failsafe does not erase the physics of impedance mismatch, and it does not declare megalithic acoustic research false for lack of mainstream agreement. It does block a jump from an illustrative reflection percentage to proof of design intent, and it flags a missing coupling path between air-borne sound and stone mass. Those two limits are adopted for this draft.

  • Supported: use of the standard impedance-mismatch expression to produce a very high energy-reflection percentage in an illustrative air–stone model; dataset statement that a piezoelectric charge calculation was unsubstantiated and removed.
  • Suggestive: the supplied arXiv context that a single subwavelength slit can show strongly enhanced transmission when boundary impedance is engineered; possible relevance to joints, slits, or cavities if, and only if, later evidence supplies geometry and coupling.
