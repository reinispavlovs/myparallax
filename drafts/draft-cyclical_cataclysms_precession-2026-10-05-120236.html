Cosmogenic nuclides and long-period comet deposition

Status: suggestive as a proposed test. The available source record reports an exploration: aluminium-26 excursions from direct bolide deposition, and carbon-14 and beryllium-10 atmospheric excursions that may result from long-period comet impacts, as distinct from ordinary cosmic-ray production. The study reports a test idea for the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis. That is consistent with an open experimental programme. It is not treated as proof that a comet impact occurred, nor as proof that orbital eccentricity paced such impacts.

Sediment platinum peak and impact versus orbital cycle

Status: unresolved for impact-versus-cycle coupling; unsubstantiated as a single combined conclusion. The skeptic critique in the dataset states that the mathematical cyclical model is illustrative, not a proven correlation mechanism, and that a sediment platinum peak at about 12800 BP indicates an external impact event that is a separate issue from the orbital-mechanics cycle. Those two items must not be conflated into one conclusion. This tribunal follows that separation: an impact-related geochemical marker, if later supported by independent measurement, would still not, by itself, establish a 25920-year eccentricity (or other orbital) catastrophe cycle.

Status: unresolved. Cyclical catastrophism remains a hypothesis cluster, not a proven mechanism, in this packet. Lack of consensus does not by itself falsify it. Claim-status summary

  • Proven: none, for a causal link between the supplied 25920-year illustrative cycle and a Younger Dryas-scale catastrophe, and none for identifying that cycle as a measured orbital-eccentricity record.
  • Supported: existence of a source record (arxiv_source_found true) that reports a cosmogenic-nuclide test idea involving bolide deposition and long-period comets; listing of 12800 BP as the packet’s Younger Dryas marker value.
  • Suggestive: the reported possibility that selected nuclide excursions could arise from comet or bolide deposition rather than only from ordinary cosmic-ray production.
