Working interpretation and limits

Taken together, the illustrative model is consistent with a near-total airborne energy reflection at a high-impedance boundary at 115.0 Hz. That outcome follows from textbook mismatch physics and does not, by itself, suggest purposeful megalithic acoustic design. The skeptic critique in the dataset is included as a required failsafe: a HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER label is not archaeological proof of intent, and airborne waves alone are not a sufficient mechanism to overcome stone mass.

A later, human-reviewed version of this draft would need independently sourced measurements of monument geometry, in situ acoustic or vibration data, and a specified coupling mechanism before any comparison with extraordinary-transmission slit models could be treated as more than an analogy. Until those sources exist in the project dataset, structural resonance of granite megaliths remains an open research question rather than a supported finding.
