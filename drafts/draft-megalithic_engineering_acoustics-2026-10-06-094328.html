Granite impedance and the reflection figure

The illustrative model reports an energy reflection of 99.98755114530057 percent at 115.0 Hz, with boundary status HIGH_REFLECTION_BARRIER, and an acoustic pressure of 28.98879093718812 Pa. The accompanying note attributes this reflection to the standard intensity-reflection form (Z1−Z2)2/(Z1+Z2)2. That attribution is consistent with ordinary acoustic impedance mismatch between a low-impedance fluid (air) and a high-impedance solid. It is not, on this dataset, a measurement taken on a named megalith, nor a reconstruction of builder intent.

Claim classification for the reflection number: supported as a textbook-style impedance-mismatch result inside an illustrative model; unsubstantiated as archaeological proof that granite architecture was designed to act as an acoustic device. The skeptic critique in the dataset is aligned with that split: a high air-to-stone reflection coefficient is expected physics, not evidence of purpose.

Extraordinary acoustic transmission (literature context)

The found arXiv-context excerpt reports that strongly enhanced transmission through a single subwavelength slit can occur when the boundary is described by an effective impedance, rather than only in periodic or multi-aperture arrays. That report is suggestive of a physical regime in which boundary-impedance engineering can reverse the usual “almost total reflection” intuition for a narrow opening. It is not a field result from granite monuments, and this draft does not treat it as showing that megalithic joints, passages, or chambers were so engineered.

Claim classification: suggestive as a laboratory or analytical acoustics result about impedance-conditioned slits; unresolved as an explanation of any particular megalithic fabric. The dataset does not supply measured slit geometry, granite surface impedance, or in-situ transmission for any structure.
