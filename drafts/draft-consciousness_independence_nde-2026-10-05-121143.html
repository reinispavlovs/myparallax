Non-local mind models versus neurological accounts

Non-local mind models treat some conscious episodes as not fully produced by, or not fully confined to, present brain activity. Reported perceptual anomalies (especially claims of veridical perception during coma, cardiac arrest, or apparent unconsciousness) are the empirical hinge for that family of models.

Neurological accounts treat the same reports as products of known or hypothesized brain states: hypoxia, REM intrusion, arousal-system discharge, migraine-related cortex, endogenous neurochemistry, or a final dream-like sequence. Several dataset titles are consistent with those accounts; they are not, in this draft, treated as a conclusive elimination of the alternative.

Project Parallax evaluation rule applied here: independent reports are data; data require sorting, not dismissal and not premature conversion into ontology. The supplied skeptic critique is aligned with that rule and is retained rather than overwritten.

Draft holding position for Project Parallax

On the supplied illustrative aggregate alone, independence of consciousness remains an unresolved empirical question. The corpus is consistent with a small classified residue of veridical-perception claims (1 record; 1.1%), a larger classified residue of neurological-mechanism papers (5 records; 5.7%), no classified null-result papers (0 records; 0.0%), and a dominant inconclusive remainder (82 records; 93.2%). Recurring reports are not zero evidence. They are also not proof. Human fact-checking should next separate on-topic NDE, coma, and end-of-life records from off-topic retrievals, replace LLM labels with dual human coding, and score only those cases that include time-locked, independently checked perceptual targets.
